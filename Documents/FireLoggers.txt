File: FireLoggersCombinedData_DRYAD.csv

Description: Data for time and temperature during fire from 48 burned sampling plots divided evenly among two burn-units, along with data for subsequent vegetation response in each of the 48 plots. Time and temperature data were measured by K-type thermocouples attached to HOBO dataloggers. A subset of plots (N=18) include time and temperature measurements in vertical profile taken: 1) 1 cm aboveground, 2) at the soil surface, 3) 1 cm belowground, 4) 2 cm belowground, and 5) 4 cm belowground.

Column Headings:
1) Observation: Each data point is given a unique observation number for reference purposes corresponding to an individual datalogger on a given day. Range = 1-120.

2) Burn.unit: Site-names (“Sunset” and “Tallwinds”) used at Camp Whispering Pines for two areas separated by fire lines burned under prescription near mid-day on two different days.

3) Plot.no: Unique 5-digit numerical IDs for each 2 x 2 m sample plot where we manipulated fine fuels.

4) Fuel.trt: Three levels of manipulated fine fuel treatments (increased-fuels, reduced-fuels, and controls) we assigned equally and randomly to 48 plots divided evenly between the two burn units.

5) Logger.pos: Position in vertical profile of thermocouple tip: Above (1cm above soil surface), Surface (on soil surface), 1Below (1 cm belowground), 2Below (2 cm belowground), 4Below (4 cm belowground), 

6) Profile: In 18 sample plots, we sampled fire temperatures in a vertical profile at 5 above- and belowground logger positions. Observations from these 18 plots are indicated with “profile” while observations from plots with just one thermocouple on the surface are indicated with “no”.

7) GreenFreq: Percentage of the 100, 10 x 10 cm cells in each 1m^2 sample plot containing any green vegetation three weeks after burning.

8) Peak.temp: Hottest temperature recorded at the given logger position during the fire. Used to calculate Maximum Temperature Increase (Max.temp.incr); units=degrees Celsius.

9) Ambient.temp: Average ambient temperature for each logger during the 30 minutes prior to the arrival of the flame front. Units=degrees Celsius.

10) Max.temp.incr: Difference between “peak.temp” and “ambient.temp” calculated for every deployed logger; units=degrees Celsius.

11) Duration.emp: Calculated duration using the time between when temperatures increased more than 0.3°C per second and the time they fell below 50°C. Calculated from “surface” and “above” loggers but used in our analyses only for “surface”; units=minutes:seconds.

12) Total.heat.emp: Area under the time-temperature series from a fire-logger during the period of “Duration.emp.”

13) Notes: Notes for specific loggers from the field, during analysis, and regarding outlier removed (n=2).
